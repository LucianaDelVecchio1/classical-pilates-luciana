# On-Page SEO, Content & Authority

Once bots can reach the page, this is where ranking is actually won: matching intent, proving
expertise, and earning trust and links. The same qualities also make a page quotable by AI.

## 1. Keyword & topic strategy (search intent)

Modern search is about *topics and intent*, not keyword density. Google and AI models
understand synonyms and concepts, so write comprehensively about a topic rather than repeating
a phrase.

- **Classify intent** for every target query and match the page format to it:
  - *Informational* ("how does X work") → guide/article/FAQ.
  - *Navigational* ("brand name login") → the specific destination page.
  - *Commercial* ("best X for Y") → comparison/review/listicle.
  - *Transactional* ("buy X", "X pricing") → product/pricing/signup page.
  Serving the wrong format is the most common reason a page won't rank no matter how good it is.
- **Build topic clusters**: a pillar page covering a broad topic, linked to cluster pages on
  subtopics, all interlinked. This signals topical authority — a major ranking lever.
- **Mine real queries**: Search Console (queries you already get impressions for), autocomplete,
  "People also ask", related searches, and — increasingly — what users actually *ask AI
  assistants*. GEO rewards optimizing for conversational, question-shaped queries.
- **Target the fan-out**: AI engines decompose a complex question into sub-queries before
  searching. "Best CRM for small agencies" fans out into "CRM pricing", "CRM features for
  agencies", "CRM integrations", etc. Cover those component questions explicitly (often as
  sections or FAQ entries) so you can be cited for each.

## 2. Title tags & meta descriptions

- **Title tag** (`<title>`): the strongest on-page relevance signal and the clickable headline
  in results. Put the primary topic near the front, keep it ~50–60 characters so it isn't
  truncated, make each unique, and write for a human (a compelling title lifts click-through
  rate, which matters). Brand at the end: `Primary Topic — Secondary | Brand`.
- **Meta description** (`<meta name="description">`): not a direct ranking factor, but it's the
  sales pitch under the title and drives CTR. ~140–160 characters, active voice, includes the
  query naturally, and states the concrete benefit. Engines may rewrite it, but a good one is
  often kept.
- Every page needs unique title + description. Duplicates are a common, easily-fixed problem.

## 3. Heading hierarchy & content structure

Structure helps humans skim, helps engines parse, and is *critical* for AI extraction.

- Exactly one `<h1>` per page = the page's main subject. Then logical `<h2>`/`<h3>` nesting;
  don't skip levels for styling (style with CSS instead).
- **Question-based headings** ("How much does X cost?") match how people query AI and voice
  search, and let a model lift your section as a direct answer.
- **Answer first, elaborate second.** Open each section with a 1–2 sentence direct answer, then
  add detail. This "inverted pyramid" is what gets pulled into featured snippets and AI answers.
- **Short paragraphs** (2–3 sentences). Use bullet lists, numbered steps, and tables for
  discrete facts, comparisons, and specs — these formats are disproportionately cited by AI and
  win rich results.
- Front-load the value: don't bury the answer under 500 words of preamble.

## 4. Content quality & E-E-A-T

Google's quality framework is **E-E-A-T**: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness,
Trustworthiness. It's not a single score but a lens raters and algorithms apply, and it maps
almost exactly onto what makes AI models trust a source.

- **Experience** — show first-hand use: original photos/screenshots, test results, case
  studies, "we ran this for 6 months and found…". Original data is gold; it's citable and
  link-worthy.
- **Expertise** — real author bylines with credentials and bios, consistent depth on the topic,
  correct terminology.
- **Authoritativeness** — be *the* recognized source: earn mentions, citations, and links from
  respected sites in your niche; branded search demand.
- **Trustworthiness** — HTTPS, clear contact/about pages, editorial and privacy policies,
  visible "last updated" dates, accurate sourced claims, real reviews. For YMYL topics (health,
  finance, legal — "Your Money or Your Life") the bar is highest.

Signals AI models specifically reward: **expert quotes with name/title/company**, **statistics
attributed to named sources** ("according to Semrush…"), clearly cited references, and dated,
current information. Write claims so they can be quoted as standalone facts.

Avoid: thin/duplicate pages, keyword stuffing, walls of unstructured text, unsourced claims,
and mass-produced AI filler — the "Helpful Content" systems demote content that exists to rank
rather than to help.

## 5. Internal linking

Often underused, high leverage.

- Link from high-authority pages (homepage, popular posts) to pages you want to boost.
- Use descriptive, varied anchor text that names the target topic (not "click here").
- Interlink within topic clusters; link new pages from existing relevant ones so they get
  discovered and inherit context.
- Keep a shallow, logical architecture so both users and crawlers can reach anything quickly.

## 6. Images & media

- Descriptive filenames (`solar-panel-efficiency-chart.webp`, not `IMG_2931.jpg`).
- Meaningful `alt` text describing the image (accessibility + image search + AI understanding).
- Compress and serve modern formats (WebP/AVIF); set explicit dimensions (CLS); lazy-load
  below-the-fold images but **not** the LCP image.
- Provide transcripts/captions for video and audio — that text is what gets indexed and cited.

## 7. Off-page authority (backlinks & brand)

Links and mentions from other sites remain among the strongest ranking signals, and AI models
lean on the same authority signals.

- **Relevance and quality over quantity.** One editorial link from a respected niche site beats
  hundreds of low-quality ones. Toxic/spammy link networks risk penalties — avoid buying links.
- **Earn links with link-worthy assets**: original research and data, free tools/calculators,
  definitive guides, expert commentary. "Digital PR" — getting cited by journalists and industry
  blogs — is the durable engine.
- **Unlinked brand mentions matter**, especially for GEO. AI models pick up on brand entities
  mentioned across the web even without a hyperlink. Getting named in articles, roundups,
  Reddit/forum threads, YouTube, and Wikipedia shapes how AI describes and recommends you.
- **Fastest GEO lift**: find the pages AI already cites for your target topics, and get your
  brand mentioned *on those pages* (guest content, expert quotes, being added to a "best of"
  list). See `geo-aeo.md`.
- Build genuine relationships and get listed in relevant, legitimate directories and industry
  associations. Consistent NAP (Name, Address, Phone) across the web matters for local SEO.
